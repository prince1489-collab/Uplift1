Why Seen exists

A note from the person who built it

I worked in a care home when I was young. There was a resident called Frank who waited for his son to turn up, every day. He'd watch the door. His son didn't come. Frank would be sad about it for a moment, then brush it off — his son must be busy.

That stayed with me. The hardest thing to watch in that building was never the illness. It was the residents who didn't hear from the people who loved them, and how much sadder that made them than anything else.

I was young and I had no idea what to do about it. But I knew I wanted to do something — for them, and for anyone who needed lifting, whatever their age.

Years later it was my turn. One winter here in the UK, I got lonely in a way I hadn't been before. I felt low, and anxious, and the days ran into each other.

What helped wasn't what I'd have guessed. It was walking the dog — not the walking, the people. A few words with complete strangers, most of whose names I never learned. Thirty seconds, no reason, nothing owed either way. I'd come home lighter.

That's when it made sense to me. The people who love you can't always be there — and Frank made excuses for that, every single day. A stranger has no reason at all to be kind to you, and that is exactly why it lands. A call you are owed is not the same as a good word you weren't.

So I built Seen.

What I want it to be

I want anyone who needs to be seen to have a window where their message is acknowledged — not by people being charitable, but by people who are there because they have been in the same place themselves, now or at some point.

That is where the name comes from. Not seen as in watched. Seen as in noticed, by someone who understands why it matters.

So there are no followers here, nothing to chase, and no argument to get pulled into. Just a way to send a little warmth to someone you will never meet, and to get some back on the days you need it.

If Seen works, then somewhere there is someone like Frank who doesn't have to brush it off today — because a stranger noticed him.

— Mahiman

Why Seen exists

A note from the founder

I worked in a care home when I was 18. There was a resident called Frank who waited for his son to turn up, every day.

He’d watch the door.

His son didn’t come.

Frank would be sad for a moment, then make an excuse for him — his son must be busy. Maybe tomorrow.

I never forgot that.

The hardest thing to witness in that building wasn’t the illness or the frailty. It was the loneliness. It was seeing people who had lived whole lives, raised families, loved people, worked, laughed, worried and cared for others — sitting there hoping someone might call or walk through the door.

And seeing how much it hurt when nobody did.

I was 18. I had no idea what I could possibly do about something that big. But I remember knowing that one day, somehow, I wanted to do something for people like Frank — and for anyone who needed lifting, whatever their age.

Years later, it was my turn to understand a little more of what loneliness feels like.

One winter here in the UK, I became lonely in a way I hadn’t experienced before. I felt low and anxious, and the days started to blur into one another.

What helped me wasn’t what I would have expected.

It was walking the dog.

Not really the walking — the people.

A few words with complete strangers. Someone saying hello. A small joke. A thirty-second conversation with someone whose name I would probably never know.

Nothing important on the surface.

But I’d come home feeling a little lighter.

And that was when something clicked.

The people who love us can’t always be there. Life gets busy. People have families, jobs, problems of their own. Frank understood that so well that he made excuses for his son every single day.

But there is something different about kindness from a stranger.

They don’t owe you anything. They have no reason to notice you, no obligation to say something kind.

And that is exactly why it can mean so much.

A call you were expecting is not quite the same as a good word you never expected to receive.

So I built Seen.

What I want it to be

I want Seen to be a place for anyone who needs to feel noticed.

A small window where you can put something into the world and know that another human being has seen it and acknowledged you.

Not out of pity. Not because they are being charitable.

But because the person on the other side understands.

Maybe they feel the same way today. Maybe they did once. Maybe they simply remember what it felt like to need somebody — anybody — to remind them that they weren’t invisible.

That is where the name comes from.

Not seen as in watched.

Seen as in noticed.

Seen as in someone, somewhere, saying: I hear you. You matter enough for me to stop for a moment.

There are no followers here. Nothing to chase. No popularity contest and no argument to get pulled into.

Just people.

A way to give a little warmth to someone you will probably never meet, and to receive some back on the days when you need it yourself.

If Seen works, then somewhere today, someone who had stopped expecting anything might hear from a stranger who had absolutely no reason to write to them.

Except that they chose to.

And sometimes, that can be enough to make a difficult day feel a little less lonely.

— Mahiman
